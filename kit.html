The Puberty Talk Starter Kit

Your free kit is ready. Print it, save it as a PDF, or come back to this page anytime.

Open conversation starters

1. Age-by-age talk scripts

Ages 7–9: lay the groundwork

  • Name body parts correctly: "Your body belongs to you, and these are the real names for its parts."
  • "In a few years your body will start to change, like everyone's does. It's called puberty. You can ask me anything about it."
  • "Surprises are fun and get shared soon. Secrets about bodies or touching are never OK to keep — always tell me."

Ages 10–11: before changes begin

  • "Some kids your age are starting to grow and change. Have you noticed anything, or heard kids talking?"
  • "Let's make a just-in-case kit for your bag — then you never have to worry."
  • "Feelings can get bigger in puberty. That's hormones, not you being 'too much.'"

Ages 12–13: while it's happening

  • "No lecture. Is there anything you've been wondering about or seen online that seemed weird?"
  • "How's your skin/sleep/sweat situation? Want to pick some products together?"
  • "If anyone ever makes you uncomfortable — online or in person — I will never be angry at you for telling me."

Ages 14+: coaching independence

  • "Would you like some time alone with the doctor at your next check-up?"
  • "Who are three adults you trust if you can't reach me?"
  • "Is there anything you wish I'd explained better?"

2. What do I say if…

  • Your child asks something you can't answer: "Great question — I'm not sure. Let's look it up together."
  • They seem embarrassed: "Lots of people feel awkward about this. We can talk while we walk or drive."
  • They heard something false at school: "I'm glad you asked me. Here's what's actually true…"
  • They start their period at school: "You did exactly the right thing. Let's restock your kit together."
  • Their friend is developing faster: "Bodies follow their own schedules. Yours is right on time for you."
  • They're worried they're 'not normal': "Tell me what you've noticed. If we have questions, we'll ask the doctor together."

3. First-period kit list

  • 2–3 pads + 1–2 liners
  • Spare underwear and dark leggings
  • Small zip bag for used items
  • Unscented wipes
  • A note with a trusted adult's number

Interactive version: Period Kit Builder.

4. Boys' puberty checklist

  • Talk about voice changes, growth spurts and sweat before they happen
  • Antiperspirant, face wash and a fresh-clothes routine
  • Explain that temporary breast swelling and unexpected erections are common and normal
  • Normalize talking about feelings — and name trusted adults
  • Check-up if no signs of puberty by 14

6. Family agreement

  • Any question is OK — nobody gets laughed at.
  • We use the real names for body parts.
  • No secrets about bodies or safety.
  • If you can't ask out loud, write it down.
  • We look things up together from trusted sources.
