Puberty is not one event but a series of changes that unfold in a fairly predictable order over several years. The exact ages vary a lot from person to person, but the sequence tends to be similar. This guide walks through what usually happens, roughly when, and how doctors track progress.

## Key takeaways

- Puberty typically begins between ages 8 and 13 for girls and 9 and 14 for boys.
- Doctors describe progress using five Tanner stages, from Stage 1 (before puberty) to Stage 5 (adult development).
- Changes follow a usual order, even though the timing differs between people.
- Girls tend to have their biggest growth spurt early in puberty, while boys tend to have theirs later.
- The whole process usually takes about 2 to 5 years.

## Why timing varies so much

Two people born on the same day can start puberty years apart, and both can be perfectly healthy. Genetics is the biggest factor, so family patterns matter. If a parent was an "early bloomer" or a "late bloomer," you may follow a similar path.

Other things can play a part, including overall health, nutrition, body weight and some long-term medical conditions. Over the past several decades, the average age of starting puberty has slowly moved earlier in many countries.

## What are Tanner stages?

Tanner stages, also called sexual maturity ratings, were developed by a British pediatrician named James Tanner. They give doctors a simple, consistent way to describe how far along someone is in puberty.

There are separate descriptions for:

- **Breast development** (girls)
- **Genital development** (boys), mainly testicle and penis size
- **Pubic hair** (everyone)

During a routine checkup, a doctor may briefly look at these areas to see which stage fits best. This is a normal, professional part of tracking healthy growth, and a parent or chaperone can usually be in the room if you prefer.

## The Tanner stages for girls

| Stage | Typical age range | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before about 8 to 11 | No visible signs of puberty yet. |
| 2 | About 8 to 13 | Breast buds form under the nipples. A small amount of fine pubic hair may appear. Growth speeds up. |
| 3 | About 9 to 14 | Breasts get larger. Pubic hair becomes darker and coarser. Fastest growth in height often happens around here. |
| 4 | About 10 to 15 | Breasts continue to fill out. Pubic hair covers more area. First period often begins in this stage. |
| 5 | About 12 to 18 | Adult breast shape and adult pubic hair pattern. Growth in height slows and stops. |

### Key milestones for girls

- **First sign:** breast buds, usually around age 10 to 11 on average.
- **Peak growth spurt:** usually about 1 to 2 years after breast buds appear.
- **First period:** usually around age 12, often 2 to 3 years after breasts start developing. Anywhere from about 10 to 15 is common.
- **Reaching adult height:** most girls reach close to their adult height within about 2 years after their first period.

## The Tanner stages for boys

| Stage | Typical age range | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before about 9 to 12 | No visible signs of puberty yet. |
| 2 | About 9 to 14 | Testicles begin to grow, and the skin around them gets thinner and slightly darker. A little fine pubic hair may appear. |
| 3 | About 11 to 15 | The penis gets longer. Pubic hair becomes darker and coarser. The voice may start to change. |
| 4 | About 12 to 16 | Further growth of the penis and testicles. Fastest growth in height usually happens around here. Underarm hair appears. |
| 5 | About 14 to 18 | Adult genital size and adult pubic hair pattern. Facial hair is more noticeable. Height growth slows. |

### Key milestones for boys

- **First sign:** the testicles get larger, usually around age 11 to 12 on average.
- **Voice changes:** often begin in the middle of puberty, around 12 to 14.
- **Peak growth spurt:** typically around age 13 to 14, later in puberty than for girls.
- **Facial hair and muscle growth:** usually develop in the later stages and can continue into the late teens or early twenties.

## A side-by-side summary

| Change | Girls (typical) | Boys (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| Puberty begins | 8 to 13 | 9 to 14 |
| Peak growth spurt | 10 to 12 | 12 to 15 |
| Signature milestone | First period, about 10 to 15 | Voice deepens, about 12 to 15 |
| Puberty mostly complete | 14 to 16 | 16 to 18 |

These numbers are averages and ranges, not deadlines. Plenty of healthy teens fall slightly outside them.

## Changes that don't follow the stages neatly

Some changes are not part of the Tanner scale but tend to show up along the way:

- **Body odor and oily skin** can start early, sometimes before other signs, because they are triggered by adrenal hormones.
- **Acne** often appears in the middle of puberty and may last into the late teens.
- **Underarm hair** usually arrives a year or two after pubic hair.
- **Emotional changes** can happen at any point and don't always match physical progress.

## When to talk to a doctor

Talk to a doctor if:

- Signs of puberty appear before age 8 in a girl or before age 9 in a boy.
- There are no signs of puberty by age 13 in a girl or age 14 in a boy.
- A girl has no period by age 15, or more than 3 years after breasts begin developing.
- Puberty seems to stall for a long time or changes appear in an unusual order.
- Growth in height seems to have stopped well before other changes are complete.

A doctor can check growth charts, ask about family history and, if needed, order simple tests. Most of the time, the answer is simply a family pattern.

## The bottom line

Puberty follows a familiar path, but everyone travels it at their own pace. The Tanner stages are just a tool to describe where you are, not a race or a scorecard. If your timing seems very early or very late, a quick check with a doctor can give you peace of mind.
