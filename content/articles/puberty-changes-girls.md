Puberty for girls usually starts somewhere between ages 8 and 13 and brings a whole series of changes over the next few years. Some are easy to see, some you mainly feel, and all of them are a normal part of growing up. Here is what each change is, why it happens and what you can do to feel comfortable.

## Key takeaways

- Most girls start puberty between 8 and 13, and the first sign is usually breast buds.
- Changes happen gradually, typically over 2 to 5 years, in a fairly predictable order.
- The first period usually comes 2 to 3 years after breasts begin to develop, often around age 12.
- Discharge, acne, body odor and mood swings are all common and normal.
- Every body develops on its own schedule, so there is no need to compare.

## What starts puberty

Puberty begins when your brain signals your ovaries to make more of the hormone **estrogen**. Estrogen drives most of the changes below. Other hormones from the adrenal glands add to things like oily skin, body odor and some hair growth.

## Breast development

Breast changes are usually the first visible sign of puberty.

- It begins with **breast buds**: small, firm lumps under the nipple. They can feel tender or itchy, and that is normal.
- Over the next few years, breasts slowly grow and change shape. The area around the nipple (the areola) may get larger and darker.
- It is very common for **one side to develop faster** than the other.
- Breasts come in many sizes and shapes, mostly decided by genetics.

A soft crop top or first bra can make you more comfortable, especially during sports, whenever you feel ready.

## Growth spurt and body shape

Girls usually have their **fastest growth early in puberty**, often between ages 10 and 12, before their first period.

During this time you might:

- Grow several centimeters in a year
- Notice your hands and feet grow first, before the rest of you
- See your hips get wider and your body shape become curvier
- Gain weight, which is a healthy and necessary part of development

Gaining some body fat around the hips, thighs and upper arms is normal and needed for healthy hormone levels and periods. It is not a sign that anything is wrong.

## Body hair

Hair growth happens in several places:

- **Pubic hair** begins as a small amount of fine hair and gradually becomes darker, coarser and curlier.
- **Underarm hair** usually appears a year or two later.
- **Leg and arm hair** may get darker or thicker.

Whether you remove body hair is entirely your personal choice. Hair has no effect on health either way.

## Vaginal discharge

Many girls notice a clear or whitish fluid in their underwear several months before their first period. This is **vaginal discharge**, and it is a healthy sign that your body is working as it should.

Normal discharge:

- Is clear, white or slightly yellowish when it dries
- Has little or a mild smell
- Can change in amount and texture over the month

Panty liners can help if you want to keep underwear fresh.

## Your first period

A period (menstruation) is when blood and tissue from the lining of the uterus leave the body through the vagina. It is a sign that the reproductive system is maturing.

| Fact | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Age at first period | About 10 to 15, average around 12 |
| Time after breast buds | About 2 to 3 years |
| Length of a period | About 2 to 7 days |
| Time between periods | About 21 to 45 days in the first years |

Periods are often **irregular for the first year or two**. Skipping a month or having cycles of different lengths is common at first.

Some girls get cramps, tiredness, bloating or mood changes just before or during a period. Gentle exercise, a warm heat pad and, with a parent's okay, over-the-counter pain relief can help.

## Skin, sweat and hygiene

Rising hormones make oil and sweat glands more active.

- **Acne** is very common. Washing your face gently twice a day and avoiding picking at spots helps.
- **Sweat and body odor** increase, especially under the arms. A daily shower and deodorant usually take care of it.
- **Hair may get oilier**, so you may want to wash it more often.

If acne is painful or affecting your confidence, a doctor can recommend effective treatments.

## Feelings and emotions

Changing hormones, a developing brain and new social situations can all affect how you feel.

You might notice:

- Mood swings or feeling more emotional than usual
- Feeling self-conscious about your body
- Wanting more privacy
- Stronger opinions and more interest in independence

These feelings are part of growing up. Sleep, movement, good food and talking to someone you trust can make a real difference.

## When to talk to a doctor

Check in with a doctor if:

- Breasts or pubic hair start developing before age 8.
- There are no signs of breast development by age 13.
- You haven't had a period by age 15, or more than 3 years after breasts started growing.
- Periods last longer than 7 days, are very heavy (soaking a pad every hour or two), or come closer than 21 days apart.
- Period pain stops you from going to school or doing normal activities.
- Discharge is green or gray, smells strong, or comes with itching or burning.
- Your mood feels low or anxious most days for two weeks or more.

## The bottom line

Puberty for girls is a gradual journey that includes breast growth, a growth spurt, new hair, discharge and eventually periods. It may feel like a lot, but each change is a normal sign of a healthy, developing body. Go at your own pace, take care of yourself and ask questions whenever something feels unclear.
