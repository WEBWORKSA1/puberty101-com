Ask anything — anonymously

Some questions are hard to say out loud. Ask here: no name, no email, no account. We answer the most common questions in plain language on the site.

Who's asking?

Under 13? Please ask a parent, caregiver or school nurse to help you. We can't reply privately — if you need help now, use a helpline below.

Questions people ask most

Is it normal for one breast to grow faster than the other?

Yes. It's very common for one side to start first or grow faster. Most even out over time, and small differences are normal into adulthood.

I'm 13 and haven't started yet. Is something wrong?

Probably not — timing varies a lot. Doctors usually suggest a check-up if girls have no breast development by 13 or boys have no testicular growth by 14, just to make sure.

How do I know my first period is coming?

Common clues: breast development for about 2–3 years, pubic hair, and white or clear discharge in your underwear for a few months. Pack a kit in your bag so you're ready.

Why does my voice keep cracking?

Your voice box is growing and your vocal cords are getting longer and thicker. The cracks are temporary and usually settle within months.

I sweat a lot and smell even after showering. What can I do?

Shower daily, wear clean clothes, and try an antiperspirant (it reduces sweat) rather than only a deodorant. Breathable fabrics help. If sweating is extreme, a doctor can help.

Is it normal to feel angry or sad for no reason?

Big, fast-changing feelings are common in puberty. Sleep, movement and talking help. If sadness lasts for weeks or you feel unsafe, tell a trusted adult or call a helpline.

Can I use a tampon for my first period?

You can, if you feel comfortable and follow the instructions, but many people start with pads. Choose the lowest absorbency you need and change it every 4–8 hours.

Do boys go through puberty later than girls?

On average, yes — by about one to two years. But individual timing varies a lot for everyone.
