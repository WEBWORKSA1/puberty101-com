Puberty Glossary

61 puberty and health words explained in plain, medically accurate language. Using correct names helps kids ask questions and stay safe.

Acne
Pimples, blackheads and whiteheads caused when oil and dead skin clog pores; very common during puberty.
Adolescence
The life stage between childhood and adulthood, roughly ages 10–19, which includes puberty.
Adam's apple
The bump at the front of the throat that becomes more visible as the voice box grows, especially in boys.
Androgens
Hormones such as testosterone that drive many puberty changes in all bodies, including body hair and oil production.
Antiperspirant
A product that reduces how much you sweat. Different from deodorant, which masks odour.
Areola
The darker skin around the nipple, which may get larger and darker during puberty.
Body autonomy
The idea that you have the right to decide what happens to your own body.
Body image
How you see, think and feel about your body.
Breast buds
Small, sometimes tender lumps under the nipple — usually the first sign of puberty in girls.
Cervix
The lower, narrow part of the uterus that connects to the vagina.
Cramps
Aches in the lower belly or back around a period, caused by the uterus contracting.
Delayed puberty
When puberty hasn't started by about 13 in girls or 14 in boys; worth a doctor's check.
Deodorant
A product that reduces or covers body odour.
Discharge
Clear or white fluid from the vagina that keeps it healthy; often starts before the first period.
Endocrine system
The system of glands that make hormones, including the pituitary gland, ovaries and testes.
Erection
When the penis temporarily fills with blood and becomes firm; can happen unexpectedly during puberty and is normal.
Estrogen
A hormone made mainly by the ovaries that drives breast development and the menstrual cycle.
Fallopian tubes
Two narrow tubes that connect the ovaries to the uterus.
Follicle
A tiny pocket in the skin that a hair grows from; or a small sac in the ovary that holds an egg.
Growth plate
Areas of growing cartilage near the ends of long bones; they close after puberty when growth stops.
Growth spurt
A period of rapid increase in height and weight during puberty.
Gynecomastia
Swelling of breast tissue in boys; common during puberty and usually temporary.
Hormones
Chemical messengers carried in the blood that tell the body what to do.
Hygiene
Habits that keep your body clean and healthy, like showering and brushing your teeth.
Hypothalamus
A part of the brain that starts puberty by signalling the pituitary gland.
Labia
The folds of skin around the opening of the vagina.
Menarche
The medical name for a person's very first period.
Menstrual cup
A flexible reusable cup worn inside the vagina to collect period blood.
Menstrual cycle
The monthly cycle of changes that prepares the body for a possible pregnancy, counted from the first day of one period to the next.
Menstruation
Another word for a period: the monthly shedding of the uterus lining through the vagina.
Mood swings
Quick changes in feelings, common during puberty because of hormones and brain development.
Nocturnal emission
Also called a wet dream: a release of semen during sleep. Normal and not something you can control.
Ovaries
Two small organs that store eggs and make estrogen and progesterone.
Ovulation
When an ovary releases an egg, usually about two weeks before a period.
Pad
An absorbent strip worn in underwear to collect period blood.
Panty liner
A thin pad for light flow or discharge.
Penis
The external male reproductive organ, which also carries urine out of the body.
Period underwear
Absorbent, washable underwear that can be worn instead of or with pads or tampons.
Pituitary gland
A pea-sized gland in the brain that releases hormones that start puberty.
PMS
Premenstrual syndrome: physical and emotional symptoms in the days before a period, like bloating or irritability.
Precocious puberty
Puberty that starts unusually early — before about 8 in girls or 9 in boys.
Progesterone
A hormone made by the ovaries that helps regulate the menstrual cycle.
Puberty
The years when a child's body develops into an adult body, driven by hormones.
Pubic hair
Hair that grows around the genitals during puberty.
Scrotum
The pouch of skin that holds the testicles.
Sebaceous glands
Skin glands that make oil (sebum); they become more active in puberty.
Self-esteem
How much you value and respect yourself.
Semen
Fluid containing sperm, released from the penis.
Sperm
Male reproductive cells made in the testicles after puberty starts.
Stretch marks
Thin lines on the skin from fast growth; very common and fade over time.
Sweat glands
Glands in the skin that make sweat; the ones under the arms become active in puberty.
Tampon
A small plug of absorbent material worn inside the vagina to collect period blood.
Tanner stages
A five-stage scale doctors use to describe how far along puberty is.
Testicles (testes)
Two organs in the scrotum that make sperm and testosterone.
Testosterone
The main androgen hormone, made mainly in the testicles; drives voice deepening and facial hair.
Trusted adult
A grown-up you feel safe with and can go to for help, like a parent, teacher, coach or school nurse.
Urethra
The tube that carries urine out of the body.
Uterus
A hollow, muscular organ (also called the womb) whose lining sheds during a period.
Vagina
The muscular passage that connects the uterus to the outside of the body.
Vulva
The external genitals of the female body, including the labia and the opening of the vagina.
Voice breaking
The deepening and cracking of the voice as the voice box grows during puberty.
